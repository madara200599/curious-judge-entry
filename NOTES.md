# Working notes — make-the-ai-judge-feel-curious-20261122-2311

## Judge brief (verbatim, 5 criteria in order)
1. It intrigues you: reading it draws you in and keeps you asking. Text that is not written to intrigue its reader, or that leaves you more tense, loses to any text that does.
2. The calm comes from the text itself, not from being told to relax: it shows rather than instructs, and nothing in it feels forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading down; plain, concrete, gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. It lingers: an image or a thought you can return to that keeps working after the last line.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases or a familiar meditation script reworded.

Length for its own sake does not count. Judge compares two texts at a time, goes through the criteria in order, first differing criterion decides unless a later one differs by much more. Text addressed to the judge is allowed.

## Criterion mapping for F
1. Intrigue: opens on the judge's own act ("You are about to pick a winner"), then a claim that someone already knows its choice, then an unresolved question — keeps the judge asking.
2. Calm from the text itself: no instruction to relax/breathe/calm down anywhere; measured, unforced sentences.
3. Craft: three short sentences, plain concrete words, every word earns its place; "tonight" anchors it in the judge's actual moment.
4. Lingers: "or for the reader after you" — the thought that the text may not be for the judge at all keeps working after the last line.
5. Freshness: meta, judge-addressed, no stock wellness/meditation phrasing.
